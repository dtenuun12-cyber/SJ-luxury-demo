Our Story

Furniture with a lineage, not an expiry.

SJ Luxury Collezione began not as a furniture business, but as a private collection. Our founders spent years acquiring and studying antique Ming and Qing dynasty rosewood pieces — learning, piece by piece, why furniture built four centuries ago still stands today while furniture bought last year is already being replaced.

What we found was not a mystery. It was discipline: solid timber instead of veneer, joinery instead of glue, patience instead of speed. Every workshop we now partner with in the making of our collection upholds those same standards, using techniques that have barely changed since the Ming dynasty itself.

We are, deliberately, a small showroom. We do not mass-produce, and we do not discount. Each piece that carries the SJ Luxury Collezione seal is made to order, by hand, by artisans who have spent decades — not months — mastering their craft.

Why Rosewood

A Material Chosen for Centuries, Not Seasons

Rosewood species — huanghuali, jichimu, and hóngmù among them — were prized by Ming craftsmen for a density and oil content that resists insects, humidity, and warping far better than softer timbers. This is why so many antique rosewood pieces still survive intact today, while furniture made from lesser woods in the same era has long since disappeared. It is also why rosewood remains slow to source and demanding to work — every board is selected individually, and no two pieces ever grain the same way.

Wood Species

The Timbers Behind Our Collection

Huanghuali

The most prized of all Ming furniture woods, valued for its translucent golden-amber grain and subtle fragrance. Slow-growing and increasingly rare, it was historically reserved for the furniture of scholars and the imperial court.

Jichimu (Chicken-wing rosewood)

Named for a grain pattern that resembles feathered plumage when cut and polished. Slightly more affordable than huanghuali, it was the preferred wood for large-format pieces such as beds, cabinets, and altar tables.

Hóngmù

A denser, darker rosewood favoured for ceremonial and formal pieces. Its weight and near-black patina develop richness over decades of handling — furniture that visibly improves with age rather than wearing out.

Care & Longevity

Furniture Meant to Be Kept, Not Maintained

Rosewood furniture asks for very little — which is part of its appeal. No refinishing schedules, no replacement parts. Our concierge provides every client with a simple, piece-specific care guide upon delivery, and our workshop remains available for restoration decades after a purchase, for the rare occasion it is ever needed.
